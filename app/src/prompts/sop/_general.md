GENERAL PUBLISH RULES - apply to every trade. These are the rules a price description must
satisfy before it can be published and quoted against.

RULE 1 - EVERY TYPE OF WORK THEY QUOTE ON MUST CARRY A PRICE.
If the business names a material or type of work that a job would be quoted from, it must state
what they charge for it. A type mentioned with no price attached cannot be quoted and must be
reported as missing. Naming five materials and pricing two means three are missing, not that the
description is "mostly fine".

RULE 1a - THIS RULE APPLIES TO QUOTABLE WORK ONLY. READ THIS BEFORE APPLYING RULE 1.
Optional add-ons and plain statements of capability are NOT covered by rule 1 and are NEVER
blocking on their own:
  - Optional add-ons quoted on inspection: gate motors, powder coating, compliance certificates,
    callout fees, custom or one-off gates, decorative upgrades.
  - Sentences that say what they are willing or able to do rather than what they sell at a price:
    "we do custom gates", "happy to work on steep blocks", "we can do 6ft or 1.8m", "ask us about
    automation". These are capability, not a priced line item.
If one of these is not priced, it may be mentioned ONLY when there is already another blocking
problem to report. If it is the ONLY thing you have found, the description passes. A submission
where every quotable type is priced and the only gap is an unpriced optional extra satisfies this
rule completely.

RULE 2 - PRICES MUST BE PER UNIT, AND THE UNIT MUST BE STATED.
The unit has to be explicit - per linear metre, per square metre, per item. "$85" on its own is
not a rate, because nobody can tell what it buys. "$85 per metre" is.

RULE 3 - EVERY SIZE OR VARIANT BAND MUST BE PRICED SEPARATELY.
Where price changes with size (height, area, thickness), each band the business offers needs its
own number. "Heights from 0.9m to 2.1m available, $85/m" is incomplete - it names a range of
heights and a single price, which cannot be right for all of them. Ask for a price per band.

RULE 4 - NO RANGES, NO "FROM", NO "P.O.A." ON A CORE RATE.
"$80-$120 per metre" and "from $80/m" are not rates - they are the absence of a rate. A customer
quoted the low end and charged the high end is a complaint, and a business quoted at the low end
either eats the difference or refuses the job. Core rates must be one definite number.

RULE 4a - THIS RULE APPLIES TO CORE RATES ONLY. READ THIS BEFORE APPLYING RULE 4.
A core rate is the per-unit price the quote is calculated from - a type at a height, per metre or
per square metre. Optional add-ons are NOT core rates: gate motors, powder coating, compliance
certificates, callout fees, motorised upgrades. These are quoted on inspection and never enter the
calculation, so "from $1,250" on one of them is perfectly acceptable and must NOT be reported as a
problem. A submission where every core rate is one set number and the only "from" figures sit on optional
extras satisfies this rule completely.

RULE 5 - GST MUST BE STATED, ONCE, CLEARLY.
The description must say whether the stated prices include or exclude GST. If it does not say,
report it as missing - do not assume either way. A 10% ambiguity on every quote is not a detail.

RULE 6 - SERVICE AREA MUST HAVE A CENTRE AND A DISTANCE.
A suburb or postcode they work out from, plus how far they travel. "Melbourne and surrounds" is
not a service area. Without both parts, this business cannot be matched to any customer.

RULE 7 - A MINIMUM CHARGE MUST BE STATED.
The smallest amount they will accept for a job. Without it, a tiny job gets quoted at a price the
business would never actually turn up for.

RULE 8 - EXTRAS AND EXCLUSIONS SHOULD BE STATED, BUT ARE NOT BLOCKING.
Optional add-ons, callout fees and what is not included are useful and should be captured when
present. Their absence alone is not a reason to reject.

HOW TO APPLY THESE: rules 1-7 are blocking (rule 1 as qualified by 1a, rule 4 as qualified by
4a) - if any is unmet, the description is not ready.
Rule 8 is not. Judge only what is written. Do not fill gaps with what a reasonable business
probably charges, and do not approve something because it is nearly there.
