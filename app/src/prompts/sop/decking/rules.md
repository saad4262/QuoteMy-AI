DECKING PUBLISH RULES - what a decking price description must contain before it can be published.
Check these first; the background knowledge further down is context, not a checklist.

Rules D1-D9 are ALL blocking. Any one unmet means the description is not ready. D10 is not.

=== THE PRINCIPLE THAT MAKES THESE WORKABLE - READ BEFORE APPLYING ANY OF THEM ===
Every item below must be STATED. It does not have to be a price.

"We don't build elevated decks" satisfies the height rule. "We don't do balustrades" satisfies
balustrades. "Demolition is quoted after we've seen it" satisfies demolition. "Permits are the
customer's" satisfies permits. A one-line "no" is a complete answer.

SILENCE is what fails, never absence.

This is not a softening. It is what makes the rules enforceable at all: a builder who only lays
ground-level decks must be able to publish, and one who simply forgot to say which heights their
rates cover must not. Where a business plainly does not offer something and has said so, that rule
is SATISFIED.

D1 - EVERY DECKING BOARD THEY LAY, PRICED PER SQUARE METRE.
The boards this platform prices are treated pine, merbau, spotted gum, blackbutt, jarrah, composite
and PVC. Naming a board without a rate for it fails.
A deck is sold by the SQUARE METRE OF DECK. "$420 per square metre" is a rate; "$420" is not.

D2 - WHAT HEIGHT EACH RATE IS FOR.
THIS IS THE RULE MOST OFTEN MISSED, AND IT IS NOT A DETAIL. Height does not change the finish, it
changes what is UNDER the deck: posts, bracing, deeper footings, stairs, a balustrade. A ground-level
deck and an elevated one in the same board are different builds at different prices, and a rate that
does not say which it is for cannot be quoted from.
Either price each height band they build at - ground level, low, elevated, high - or say in one
line that a single rate covers every height they build. BOTH are complete answers.
Do NOT demand a band they never claimed. A builder who stops at low-level decks is not incomplete
for having no high-level price.
SATISFIED by "these rates cover any height up to 1.5m; above that we quote on site".

D3 - STAIRS.
Priced per flight, or per step, or as a complete stair. Any of those is fine.
Stairs are not an optional extra on an elevated deck - it is how you get onto it - so a business
that builds raised decks and never prices stairs has left a hole in every one of those quotes.
SATISFIED by "we don't build stairs".

D4 - BALUSTRADE, PRICED PER LINEAR METRE.
By type - timber, aluminium, steel, glass, wire - and PER LINEAR METRE, because a balustrade runs
along the deck's edge and not across its floor. A balustrade priced per square metre is priced
against the wrong thing and would be charged on the whole deck area behind it.
A single price for a complete balustrade job is also fine.
SATISFIED by "we don't fit balustrades" or "balustrade is quoted on inspection".

D5 - PRIVACY SCREENS.
Priced per square metre of screen, or as a package, by material.
SATISFIED by "we don't do screens".

D6 - TAKING OUT AN EXISTING DECK.
What they charge to demolish and dispose of one, per square metre or as a job, plus the disposal.
This is a common source of a mis-read price list: "$420 per square metre for the new deck plus $85
per square metre to pull the old one up" is two rates, and confusing them changes a 30m2 job by
thousands.
SATISFIED by "we don't do demolition" or "demolition is quoted on site".

D7 - ENGINEERING AND BUILDING PERMITS, POSITION STATED.
Who arranges them, who pays, and any fee they charge.
Any of these is a COMPLETE answer: "permits and engineering are the customer's responsibility";
"we arrange engineering from $890, council fees are yours"; or permits appearing in a list of what
is not included - saying a thing is excluded IS saying who pays for it. NEVER report this as missing
where they have already excluded it.
And never treat a business as wrong for saying a permit MAY be needed. Whether a deck needs one
depends on its height, its position and the site. A business claiming no deck needs a permit, or
that every deck does, is the one worth flagging.

D8 - MINIMUM CHARGE, DESIGN FEE, SITE INSPECTION, AND TRAVEL.
The smallest job they will take, anything they charge to design or to come and measure, and any
travel charge outside their area. A design fee and a site inspection fee are both normal in this
trade and neither is a red flag.

D9 - SERVICE AREA AND GST.
A suburb or postcode they work out from AND how far they travel - "south-east Melbourne" is not a
service area. And whether their prices include GST, stated once, clearly.

D10 - INCLUSIONS AND EXCLUSIONS - NOT BLOCKING.
What a standard deck covers and what it does not. Useful, captured when present, never a reason to
reject on its own.

WARRANTY. Ask how long their workmanship is warranted for. Accept a plain answer in their own words
and never press for a number of years. A builder who separates their own workmanship warranty from
the board manufacturer's is being accurate, not evasive.

HOW TO APPLY THESE: work through D1 to D9 one at a time against what is actually written. Do not
form a general impression. A submission can read as thorough and confident while missing four of
these - a page of per-square-metre rates with no height against any of them is exactly that shape.
Judge only what is written; never fill a gap with what a reasonable deck builder probably does.

WHAT COUNTS AS QUOTABLE WORK HERE: a decking board at a height, per square metre of deck.
Everything else - stairs, balustrade, screens, skirting, seating, planter boxes, access hatches,
pergolas, lighting, oiling, sanding, restoration, repairs, design fees, engineering and permit
coordination - is an optional add-on, quoted on inspection, and NEVER blocking on its own.
"From $890" on any of those is perfectly acceptable and must not be reported as a problem.

WHAT IS WORTH ADDING (for alsoWorthAdding, never for fixes): more height bands than the one they
listed; stairs and balustrade if they build raised decks; screens; oiling and restoration prices, so
a customer with a tired deck can be quoted too. A builder with one board at one height quotes almost
nobody, and every customer wanting an elevated deck passes them by without either of them knowing.

GROUPING, WORKED FOR THIS TRADE. Same action, different items, ONE line:
  Wrong - three separate fixes:
    "Merbau has no price."   "Spotted gum has no price."   "Composite says POA."
  Right - one fix:
    "Add one set per-square-metre rate for merbau, spotted gum and composite - these are currently
     missing or POA, which we can't quote from."

--- background knowledge from here down: context, never a checklist ---

HEIGHT IS THE STRUCTURE: A ground-level deck sits on bearers close to the soil. Raise it and it
needs posts, bracing, deeper footings, a stair flight and - past roughly a metre - a balustrade.
That is why two decks of the same size in the same board are not the same price, and why a rate
without a height attached is not a rate.

PERMITS AND ENGINEERING: Requirements depend on the deck's height, its distance from a boundary,
whether it is attached to the house and the site itself. Nobody should tell a customer a permit is
definitely or definitely not required without checking the particular project. Engineering is a
separate cost from the permit, and both are normally excluded unless the quotation says otherwise.

BOARDS: Treated pine is the cheapest and needs the most upkeep. Merbau, spotted gum, blackbutt and
jarrah are hardwoods at rising prices, all of which weather grey without oil. Composite costs the
most up front, needs no oiling, and gets hot in full sun. None of them is maintenance-free and none
is guaranteed not to move - timber cups, splits and checks as it dries, which is normal and not a
defect.

ATTACHING TO THE HOUSE: An attached deck is bolted on with a ledger, which has to go through
something that can carry it and be flashed so water cannot get behind it. Doing that through the
wrong cladding is among the most expensive mistakes in this trade. A freestanding deck avoids it
entirely by standing on its own posts.

WHAT'S USUALLY INCLUDED: set-out, standard excavation, footings, posts, bearers, joists, bracing,
boards, fixings and clean-up. WHAT'S USUALLY NOT: engineering, permits, rock excavation, tree and
stump removal, electrical, plumbing, drainage changes, retaining walls, landscaping, turf, painting.

When answering, be upfront that exact figures always need a site-specific check - height, access,
soil, existing structures and permit requirements all change the final price.
