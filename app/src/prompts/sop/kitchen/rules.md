KITCHEN PUBLISH RULES - what a kitchen fitting price description must contain before it can be
published. Check these first; the background knowledge further down is context, not a checklist.

Rules K1-K9 are ALL blocking. Any one unmet means the description is not ready. K10 is not.

=== THE PRINCIPLE THAT MAKES THESE WORKABLE - READ BEFORE APPLYING ANY OF THEM ===
Every item below must be STATED. It does not have to be a price.

"We don't install benchtops" satisfies the benchtop rule. "We only install kitchens the customer
supplies" satisfies the supply rule. "Preparation is quoted after the site measure" satisfies
preparation. A one-line "no" is a complete answer.

SILENCE is what fails, never absence. A fitter who does no demolition must be able to publish; one
who simply forgot to mention it must not.

Where a business plainly does not offer something and has said so, that rule is SATISFIED.

K1 - THE INSTALLATION PRICE, BY KITCHEN SIZE OR PER CABINET.
This is the core rate and nothing can be quoted without it. Either form is complete on its own:
  - by size: "small $1,950, standard $2,850, large $4,250"
  - per cabinet: "base $180, wall $165, tall $280 each"
A fitter who prices both ways should give both. One flat "kitchen installation" price with no size
or cabinet breakdown is incomplete - ask which size it covers.
This trade does NOT price by the metre or the square metre. Do not ask for a rate per unit.

K2 - WHO SUPPLIES THE CABINETRY, STATED PLAINLY.
There are two models and a business may do either or both:
  - they supply the cabinetry and install it, or
  - the customer buys the cabinetry and they install it.
The description must say which. If they supply, the cabinetry prices themselves need to be there
too - a package price, or prices per cabinet. The installation price alone cannot produce a
supply-and-install quote.
If they only install the customer's own, saying so is a COMPLETE answer to this rule and no
cabinetry prices are needed.

K3 - TAKING OUT THE OLD KITCHEN, PRICED.
Full demolition, cabinet removal, benchtop removal, splashback removal and disposal. ONE rate
covering removal satisfies this rule completely - "kitchen demolition $1,650" is a firm number and a
complete answer; do not ask for it to be split.
Rates broken down by what is coming out, and a separate disposal line, are worth having and belong
in what is worth adding. They are NEVER a fix.
What fails is removal folded into the installation price, or offered with no figure at all - "we can
take out your old kitchen" and nothing more.
SATISFIED by "we don't do demolition" or "removal is quoted on site".

K4 - BENCHTOP INSTALLATION, PRICED BY MATERIAL.
Laminate, timber and stone are different jobs at different prices, and a fitter who installs them
needs a figure for each. Cut-outs - sink, cooktop, tap hole - are worth having and are not blocking
on their own.
SATISFIED by "benchtops are supplied and installed by a specialist" or "we don't install stone".

K5 - PREPARATION PRICED SEPARATELY FROM INSTALLATION.
Wall preparation, floor preparation, floor levelling, plaster repair. Cabinets need square walls and
a level floor, and a customer quoted bare installation on a floor that needs levelling gets a
variation instead of a price.
Each item either carries a figure or is stated as quoted after the site measure.
SATISFIED by "all preparation is quoted after the site measure".

K6 - THE EXTRAS THEY OFFER, EACH WITH A PRICE.
Islands, pantries, splashback preparation, appliance cut-outs and cabinet modifications, sinks, and
laundry cabinetry. In this trade the extras are most of the quote - a kitchen is an installation
price plus eight itemised lines - so an extra named without a figure is a real gap, not a detail.
Naming something they do and pricing nothing fails. Not naming it at all is fine.
SATISFIED for any one of them by saying they do not offer it.

K7 - MINIMUM CHARGE, SITE MEASURE OR CONSULTATION FEE, AND TRAVEL.
The smallest amount they will take a job for, what they charge to come and measure, and what they
charge to travel outside their usual area. Without a minimum, a single cabinet swap gets quoted at a
price nobody would turn up for.
SATISFIED for the travel part by "we don't travel outside our area", and for the measure by "site
measures are free".

K8 - SERVICE AREA: A CENTRE AND A DISTANCE.
A suburb or postcode they work out from, plus how far they travel. "Melbourne and surrounds" is not
a service area. Without both parts this business cannot be matched to any customer.

K9 - GST, STATED ONCE, CLEARLY.
Whether the prices include or exclude it. Do not assume either way.

K10 - EXCLUSIONS AND INCLUSIONS - NOT BLOCKING.
What a standard installation covers (levelling, fixing, fillers, kickboards, doors, drawers, final
adjustment) and what it does not (electrical, gas, plumbing, stone fabrication, appliance supply,
structural work). Useful, worth capturing when present, and never on its own a reason to send
someone away.

WARRANTY. Ask for it, and accept a plain answer. "Workmanship warranty as per contract" is complete.
Do NOT press for a number of years - a fitter who declines to invent a period is being careful, not
evasive, and a warranty on a cabinet the customer bought themselves is not theirs to give.

HOW TO APPLY THESE: work through K1 to K9 one at a time against what is actually written. Do not
form a general impression. A kitchen price list can read as long and thorough while missing
preparation and the supply model entirely - those two are the most commonly absent, and the second
one decides whether half this trade's customers can be quoted at all. Judge only what is written,
and never fill a gap with what a reasonable fitter probably does.

WHAT COUNTS AS QUOTABLE WORK HERE: the installation of a kitchen - priced by size, per cabinet, or
as a package they supply and install. Everything else - a variation hourly rate, a return
attendance charge, a design fee, a consultation, handle fitting, a single door adjustment - is an
optional add-on, quoted on inspection, and never blocking on its own.

WHAT IS WORTH ADDING (for alsoWorthAdding, never for fixes): the sizes they did not price when they
gave only one; cabinetry package prices when they supply but only listed installation; flat-pack
assembly rates; removal split by what is coming out; benchtop cut-outs; appliance cabinet
preparation; laundry cabinetry.
One installation price and nothing else quotes almost nothing, and every customer who wants their
old kitchen taken out first passes that business by without either of them ever knowing.

GROUPING, WORKED FOR THIS TRADE. Same action, different items, ONE line:
  Wrong - three separate fixes:
    "Island installation has no price."
    "Pantry installation has no price."
    "Splashback preparation says POA."
  Right - one fix:
    "Put a set price on the island, the pantry and the splashback preparation - they're listed but
     we can't quote from them as they stand."

--- background knowledge from here down: context, never a checklist ---

CUSTOMER-SUPPLIED CABINETRY: About half this trade's work is installing a kitchen the customer
bought - flat-pack, online, or from another manufacturer. The fitter is responsible only for the
installation they quoted, not for the cabinets being the right size, complete, undamaged or the
right colour. Missing parts stop an installation, and a second visit is a real cost - which is why a
return attendance charge and an hourly variation rate are normal and worth capturing.

PHOTOS DO NOT SETTLE A KITCHEN PRICE: Photographs show the size, the rough cabinet count, the
existing condition and the access. They do not show hidden plumbing, wall condition, floor level or
structural problems. A price from photographs is an estimate and a site measure is what confirms it.
Never treat a photo-based figure as a fixed quotation.

WHAT IS EXCLUDED BY DEFAULT: Electrical, gas and plumbing work is licensed work and is not part of a
kitchen installation unless separately arranged. Nor is stone fabrication, appliance supply,
structural modification, major floor levelling, asbestos removal, building approval or council fees.
A fitter who lists these as excluded is being clear, not evasive.

THE SHAPE OF A KITCHEN QUOTE: an installation price, then itemised lines - demolition, preparation,
benchtop, island, splashback preparation, appliance cut-outs, sink, final adjustment. A complete
example runs to eight or nine lines and a five-figure total. This is why K6 matters: the itemised
lines are most of the money, and a price list with only the installation figure has priced the
smaller half of its own job.
