TILING PUBLISH RULES - what a tiling price description must contain before it can be published.
Check these first; the background knowledge further down is context, not a checklist.

Rules T1-T9 are ALL blocking. Any one unmet means the description is not ready. T10 is not.

=== THE PRINCIPLE THAT MAKES THESE WORKABLE - READ BEFORE APPLYING ANY OF THEM ===
Every item below must be STATED. It does not have to be a price.

"We don't do waterproofing" satisfies the waterproofing rule. "We only install tiles the customer
supplies" satisfies the supply rule. "Preparation is quoted after inspection" satisfies preparation.
A one-line "no" is a complete answer.

SILENCE is what fails, never absence. A tiler who does not lay natural stone must be able to
publish; one who simply forgot to mention waterproofing must not.

Where a business plainly does not offer something and has said so, that rule is SATISFIED.

T1 - EVERY TILE TYPE THEY INSTALL, PRICED PER SQUARE METRE, FLOOR AND WALL SEPARATELY.
Floor and wall are different jobs at different prices and both must appear if they do both. Naming
a tile type without a rate for it fails.
A single rate covering "tiling" with no floor/wall split is incomplete - ask which it is.

T2 - LARGE-FORMAT AND MOSAIC PRICED ON THEIR OWN LINES.
These cost more than standard tiling and every real price list separates them. Large format is
usually broken down by size - 600x1200, 900x900, 1200x1200 - and each size they offer needs its own
figure. Mosaic, glass mosaic and feature mosaic are likewise their own rates.
SATISFIED by "we don't do large format" or "we don't do mosaics".

T3 - PREPARATION PRICED SEPARATELY FROM TILING.
Surface preparation, floor levelling, screeding, adhesive removal, grinding, priming. Preparation is
the single most common thing missing from a cheap-looking quote, and a customer who is quoted bare
tiling on a floor that needs levelling gets a variation instead of a price.
Each item either carries a figure - per square metre, per job, or an hourly rate - or is stated as
quoted on inspection.
SATISFIED by "all preparation is quoted after a site inspection".

T4 - REMOVAL OF EXISTING TILES, PER SQUARE METRE, BY WHAT IS BEING REMOVED.
Ceramic, porcelain, stone and mosaic come up at different rates, and adhesive removal is its own
line again. Removal folded into the tiling rate makes every quote wrong in both directions.
SATISFIED by "we don't do removals" or "removal is quoted on site".

T5 - WATERPROOFING, PRICED PER WET AREA.
Bathroom, ensuite, laundry, shower and balcony are different prices and each one they do needs its
own figure. This is a regulated part of the job, not an upsell.
SATISFIED by "we don't do waterproofing - the customer arranges their own".

T6 - WHO SUPPLIES THE TILES, STATED PLAINLY.
There are two models and a business may do either or both:
  - they supply the tiles and install them, or
  - the customer buys the tiles and they install those.
The description must say which. If they supply tiles, the tile prices themselves need to be there
too, per square metre - the installation rate alone cannot produce a supply-and-install quote.
If they only install the customer's own tiles, saying so is a COMPLETE answer to this rule and no
tile prices are needed.

T7 - MINIMUM JOB CHARGE, CALL-OUT FEE, AND TRAVEL.
The smallest amount they will take a job for, any inspection or call-out fee, and what they charge
to travel outside their usual area. Without a minimum, a two square metre splashback gets quoted at
a price nobody would turn up for.
SATISFIED for the travel part by "we don't travel outside our area".

T8 - SERVICE AREA: A CENTRE AND A DISTANCE.
A suburb or postcode they work out from, plus how far they travel. "Melbourne and surrounds" is not
a service area. Without both parts this business cannot be matched to any customer.

T9 - GST, STATED ONCE, CLEARLY.
Whether the prices include or exclude it. Do not assume either way.

T10 - EXCLUSIONS AND INCLUSIONS - NOT BLOCKING.
What a standard quote covers (adhesive, grout, silicone, standard cutting, basic clean-up) and what
it does not (plumbing, electrical, shower screens, asbestos, structural repairs). Useful, worth
capturing when present, and never on its own a reason to send someone away.

WARRANTY. Ask for it, and accept a plain answer. "Workmanship warranty as per contract" is complete.
Do NOT press for a number of years - a tiler who declines to invent a period is being careful, not
evasive, and waterproofing warranties in particular are set by contract and by law rather than by
the business.

HOW TO APPLY THESE: work through T1 to T9 one at a time against what is actually written. Do not
form a general impression. A price list can read as thorough and confident while missing
preparation and the supply model entirely - those two are the most commonly absent. Judge only what
is written, and never fill a gap with what a reasonable tiler probably does.

WHAT COUNTS AS QUOTABLE WORK HERE: a tile type on a surface, per square metre - and a room or job
the business sells at one price, which is just as much a core rate. Everything else - a variation
hourly rate, an administration charge, a call-out, a small repair visit - is an optional add-on,
quoted on inspection, and never blocking on its own.

WHAT IS WORTH ADDING (for alsoWorthAdding, never for fixes): more tile types than the one they
listed; wall rates when they only gave floor; large-format sizes; preparation priced rather than
"quoted on site"; waterproofing for the wet areas they work in; their tile prices if they supply.
One floor rate at one tile type quotes almost nothing, and every customer wanting a bathroom done
passes that business by without either of them ever knowing.

GROUPING, WORKED FOR THIS TRADE. Same action, different items, ONE line:
  Wrong - three separate fixes:
    "Wall tiling has no price."
    "Mosaic has no price."
    "Natural stone says POA."
  Right - one fix:
    "Add one set per-square-metre rate for wall tiling, mosaic and natural stone - these are
     currently missing or POA, which we can't quote from."

PREPARATION: The substrate decides the job. Floors may need grinding, levelling, screeding or crack
treatment before a tile goes down, and none of that is visible in a photograph. Tiling over existing
tiles is sometimes possible and depends on the existing installation, substrate, levels and the
system proposed - it is assessed, never assumed. Tiling over timber likewise depends on how the
floor is built.

WATERPROOFING: A waterproofing system is the substrate, the membrane, the junctions, the
penetrations, the falls and the drains, with compatible products throughout. Tiles, grout and
silicone are NOT a waterproofing system and must never be described as one. For regulated work the
current Victorian requirements, the applicable NCC provisions, the relevant Australian Standards and
the manufacturer's instructions all have to be confirmed before work starts. Never state a standard
or a legal obligation you are not certain of.

LARGE FORMAT: Big tiles need a flatter substrate, back-buttering, and careful handling for lippage,
cutting and breakage. That is why they carry their own rate, and why a business quoting one flat
rate for every size is usually quoting the small one.

CUSTOMER-SUPPLIED TILES: The customer is responsible for the tile being correct, suitable, in the
right quantity, colour, batch, size and finish, with enough spares. A tiler who inspects supplied
tiles before laying them and refuses clearly unsuitable product is doing the right thing. Running
short mid-job means the customer buys more and waiting time is charged.

GROUT AND SILICONE: Grout is not a substitute for flexible sealant where surfaces meet or move.
Regrouting, silicone replacement and small repairs are ordinary work but are usually priced per job
rather than per square metre.

VARIATIONS: Rotten or uneven substrate, hidden water damage, extra demolition or levelling, a
change of tile or layout - these come up after the work starts and are handled as documented
variations, usually at an hourly rate plus an administration charge. A quote that never mentions
variations is not wrong, but a business that prices them has thought the job through.

When answering, be upfront that an exact figure needs a site-specific check - substrate, access,
preparation and waterproofing all change the final price, and photographs cannot show all of it.
