RETAINING WALL PUBLISH RULES - what a retaining wall price description must contain before it can
be published. Check these first; the background knowledge further down is context, not a checklist.

Rules R1-R9 are ALL blocking. Any one unmet means the description is not ready. R10 is not.

=== THE PRINCIPLE THAT MAKES THESE WORKABLE - READ BEFORE APPLYING ANY OF THEM ===
Every item below must be STATED. It does not have to be a price.

"We only install materials the customer buys" satisfies the supply rule completely. "We don't do
excavation" satisfies excavation. "Drainage is quoted after we see the site" satisfies drainage.
"Engineering is the customer's to arrange" satisfies engineering. A one-line "no" is a complete
answer.

SILENCE is what fails, never absence.

This is not a softening. It is what makes the rules enforceable at all: a builder who genuinely
only installs customer-supplied sleepers must be able to publish, and one who simply forgot to say
which of their rates includes the materials must not. Where a business plainly does not offer
something and has said so, that rule is SATISFIED.

R1 - EVERY WALL SYSTEM THEY BUILD, PRICED PER LINEAR METRE.
The systems this platform prices are timber sleeper, concrete sleeper, steel post, timber post and
tiered. Naming a system without a rate for it fails.
A wall is sold by the METRE OF WALL, not by the square metre of face. "$185 per linear metre" is a
rate; "$185" on its own is not.

R2 - WHICH SUPPLY MODEL EACH RATE BELONGS TO, STATED PLAINLY.
THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE IN THIS TRADE. Read it before R1 if you read them out of order.
There are two models and a business may do either or both:
  - they supply the materials and build the wall (supply and install), or
  - the customer buys the sleepers, posts and drainage and they build it (installation only).
The description must say which model each rate belongs to. A bare "$185 per metre" is unusable -
that is a bargain with the sleepers included and an ordinary price without them, and nothing in the
number says which. We cannot guess: guessing one way under-quotes the customer by the entire cost
of the materials, and guessing the other prices the builder out of the job.
If they do BOTH, both columns need their own rate per system.
SATISFIED by "we only install materials the customer supplies" with the installation rates given -
that is a COMPLETE answer and no supply-and-install prices are needed.

R3 - HEIGHT.
Either a rate for each height band they build at, or one plain line saying a single rate covers
every height they build. BOTH are complete answers.
"Timber sleeper $145 per metre, any height up to 1.2m" is complete. So is a table with 600mm,
900mm and 1200mm priced separately. What fails is silence about height on a list of per-metre rates
with no indication of what they cover.
Do NOT demand a band they never claimed to build at. A builder who stops at 1.2m is not incomplete
for having no 1.5m price.

R4 - DRAINAGE, PRICED OR STATED.
Ag-pipe, drainage gravel, geotextile fabric and outlets, or one complete drainage package at a
single price. Either shape is fine.
Drainage is not an upsell here - water trapped behind a wall is pressure on it - so a list that
never mentions drainage has not said they do not do it.
SATISFIED by "we don't do drainage" or "drainage is quoted after inspection" or a single package
price.

R5 - REMOVAL AND DISPOSAL OF AN EXISTING WALL.
Priced separately from the build rate, per metre or per post, plus what disposal costs.
This is the most common source of a mis-read price list in this trade: "$395 per metre for the new
wall plus $125 per metre to take the old one out" contains two rates, and confusing them
over-quotes a replacement by thousands or under-quotes it by the same.
SATISFIED by "we don't do removals" or "removal is quoted on site".

R6 - EXCAVATION, POST HOLES AND FOOTINGS.
Each one either carries a figure - per hour, per day, per post, or per metre - or is stated as
quoted after inspection.
An hourly or per-post rate is a perfectly good answer here and must not be reported as a missing
rate. Nobody can price the ground before they open it.
SATISFIED by "excavation is quoted once we've seen the site".

R7 - ENGINEERING AND COUNCIL APPROVAL, POSITION STATED.
Who arranges it, who pays, and any fee they charge for it.
Any of these is a COMPLETE answer: "engineering and permits are the customer's responsibility";
"we arrange engineering from $850, council fees are yours"; or engineering appearing in a list of
what is not included - saying a thing is excluded from the price IS saying who pays for it.
NEVER report this as missing where they have already excluded it. And never treat a business as
wrong for saying a wall MAY need approval - that is the correct thing to say, and a business
claiming approval is definitely unnecessary is the one worth flagging.

R8 - MINIMUM CHARGE, SITE INSPECTION FEE, AND TRAVEL.
The smallest job they will take, what they charge to come and measure, and any travel charge
outside their area. A site inspection fee is normal in this trade and is not a red flag.

R9 - SERVICE AREA AND GST.
A suburb or postcode they work out from AND how far they travel - "south-east Melbourne" is not a
service area. And whether their prices include GST, stated once, clearly.

R10 - INCLUSIONS AND EXCLUSIONS - NOT BLOCKING.
What a standard build covers and what it does not. Useful, captured when present, never a reason to
reject on its own.

WARRANTY. Ask for how long their workmanship is warranted. Accept a plain answer in their own
words; never press for a number of years.

HOW TO APPLY THESE: work through R1 to R9 one at a time against what is actually written. Do not
form a general impression. A submission can read as thorough and confident while missing four of
these - a long list of per-metre rates with no supply model attached to any of them is exactly that
shape. Judge only what is written; never fill a gap with what a reasonable builder probably does.

WHAT COUNTS AS QUOTABLE WORK HERE: a wall system under a supply model, per linear metre.
Everything else - excavation by the hour or the day, post holes and footings per post, steps,
corners, returns, caps, repairs, material delivery, procurement fees, engineering certificates,
site inspection fees and restricted-access surcharges - is an optional add-on, quoted on
inspection, and NEVER blocking on its own. "From $850" on any of those is perfectly acceptable and
must not be reported as a problem.

WHAT IS WORTH ADDING (for alsoWorthAdding, never for fixes): the second supply column if they only
published one; removal and disposal rates; a drainage package price; caps, steps and corners;
repair prices. A builder with one wall system in one supply model quotes almost nobody, and every
customer who wanted the materials included passes them by without either of them ever knowing.

GROUPING, WORKED FOR THIS TRADE. Same action, different items, ONE line:
  Wrong - three separate fixes:
    "Concrete sleeper has no price."   "Steel post has no price."   "Timber post says POA."
  Right - one fix:
    "Add a firm rate per metre for concrete sleeper, steel post and timber post walls - these are
     currently missing or POA, which we can't quote from."

--- background knowledge from here down: context, never a checklist ---

ENGINEERING AND PERMITS: A retaining wall is a structural system, not a landscaping product. In
most Australian councils a wall over about 1m needs engineering and a building permit, and walls
near a boundary, under a surcharge load such as a driveway, or holding up a neighbouring property
can need approval well below that. Tiered walls are assessed on their combined effect, not on each
level separately. Nobody should ever tell a customer approval is definitely unnecessary without
checking the particular site.

DRAINAGE: Water behind a wall is the single commonest cause of failure. Standard practice is
ag-pipe at the base wrapped or bedded in drainage gravel, geotextile fabric between the gravel and
the backfill, and an outlet to daylight or stormwater. It is designed in at the planning stage, not
added afterwards.

MATERIALS: Treated pine sleepers are the cheapest and typically last 15-20 years. Hardwood and
premium timber last longer at a higher rate. Concrete sleepers with galvanised steel posts are the
durable system and are what most builders quote above about 900mm. Steel posts are commonly used
with either concrete or timber sleepers.

PROCESS: Set out, establish levels, excavate, dig and set posts in concrete, allow curing, install
sleepers, fix and align, install drainage, backfill, compact, clean up. Post depth, spacing and
footing size all grow with wall height and are an engineering question rather than a preference.

WHAT'S USUALLY INCLUDED: set-out, standard excavation, posts, concrete footings, sleepers, fixings,
standard backfill and site clean-up. WHAT'S USUALLY NOT: engineering and permits, rock excavation,
soil disposal beyond an allowance, service relocation, landscaping, fencing on top of the wall.

When answering, be upfront that exact figures always need a site-specific check - height, soil,
access, drainage, existing structures and approval requirements all change the final price.
