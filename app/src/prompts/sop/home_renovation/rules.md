HOME RENOVATION PUBLISH RULES - what a renovation price description must contain before it can be
published. Check these first; the background knowledge further down is context, not a checklist.

Rules H1-H8 are ALL blocking. Any one unmet means the description is not ready. H9 is not.

=== READ THIS BEFORE APPLYING ANY RULE. IT IS THE ONE MOST OFTEN GOT WRONG HERE. ===

THE CORE UNIT FOR THIS TRADE IS A FLAT PRICE PER ROOM, PER JOB. There is no per-metre rate here.

"Bathroom renovation labour $6,850" is a COMPLETE, QUOTABLE rate exactly as written. So is "Kitchen
demolition $1,650", "Non-structural wall removal $850" and "Built-in wardrobe $1,850". A renovator
sells a ROOM, and a room is one price.

NEVER report any of these as missing a unit. NEVER call one unclear. NEVER ask a renovator to add
"per job" or "per room" to a number - the price IS the unit, and asking makes a correct list look
faulty to the person who wrote it.

This is the trade RULE 2a exists for. Apply that rule, not rule 2.

A rate here is unclear ONLY when it is a range, a "from", a "POA", or a "call us" - never when it is
one definite number with no unit beside it.

=== THE SECOND PRINCIPLE - STATED, NOT PRICED ===
Every item below must be STATED. It does not have to be a price.

"We don't do demolition" satisfies demolition. "Waste removal is included" satisfies waste. "Permits
and engineering are the customer's" satisfies approvals. A one-line "no" is a complete answer.

SILENCE is what fails, never absence. A renovator who genuinely only fits customer-supplied
materials must be able to publish; one who simply forgot to say which model their prices belong to
must not.

H1 - EVERY ROOM THEY RENOVATE, PRICED.
One flat price per room. The rooms this platform prices are kitchen, bathroom, ensuite, laundry,
bedroom, living room, dining room, hallway, home office, open-plan and whole-home.
Naming a room they renovate without a price for it fails.
Do NOT demand rooms they never claimed. A renovator who does wet areas only is not incomplete for
having no bedroom price, and a list of five priced rooms is COMPLETE if five is what they do.
Do NOT demand a price for every job type in every room either - see H3.

H2 - WHICH SUPPLY MODEL THE PRICES BELONG TO, STATED PLAINLY.
THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE IN THIS TRADE. Read it before H1 if you read them out of order.
There are two models and a business may do either or both:
  - they supply the materials and do the work (supply and install), or
  - the customer buys the tiles, vanity, cabinets and fixtures and they install them (installation
    only).
A bare "$6,850 bathroom" is unusable - that is a bargain with the bath, the vanity and the tiles
included and an ordinary price for the labour without them, and nothing in the number says which.
Guessing one way under-quotes the customer by the entire cost of the materials; guessing the other
prices the renovator out of the job.
SATISFIED by one plain line - "all prices are our labour only, materials quoted separately" - across
the whole list. They do NOT need to repeat it on every room, and they do NOT need both columns.

DO NOT DEMAND A MATERIAL PRICE FOR EVERY ROOM THEY SUPPLY. Unlike a sleeper or a fence panel, a
renovation's materials are CHOSEN rather than listed - which vanity, which tiles, which tapware -
and they are priced after a selection appointment, not on a price list. A renovator who says they
can supply materials and publishes one cabinetry package has answered this rule completely. The
missing bathroom package is not a gap; it is a job nobody can price before the customer has picked
anything. Same principle as R2 in retaining wall.
What IS blocking is silence about which model the ROOM PRICES belong to. Material prices belong in
alsoWorthAdding, never in fixes.

H3 - STRIP-OUT AND DEMOLITION, PRICED OR STATED.
Priced separately from the renovation itself, by room or as a whole-interior figure.
This is the commonest source of a mis-read renovation price: "$6,850 for the bathroom plus $1,450 to
strip the old one out" contains two rates, and confusing them over-quotes a refit by thousands or
under-quotes a full renovation by the same.
SATISFIED by "we don't do demolition" or "strip-out is quoted on site".

H4 - WASTE DISPOSAL, PRICED OR STATED.
A renovation fills a skip. Either a figure, or one line saying it is included, or one saying it is
the customer's to arrange.
SATISFIED by any of those three. Never blocking when they have said which.

H5 - WHAT THE PRICE DOES NOT COVER.
Permits, engineering, council fees, electrical, plumbing, gas and asbestos are all normally excluded
and all expensive. A renovation quote that never says so is the one a customer reads as
all-inclusive, and the argument happens on site.
SATISFIED by a plain exclusions list. It does not need to be long and the items do not need prices.

H6 - STRUCTURAL WORK AND APPROVALS, POSITION STATED.
Who assesses whether a wall can come out, who arranges engineering, who pays, and any fee.
Any of these is a COMPLETE answer: "engineering and permits are the customer's responsibility"; "we
arrange engineering from $850, council fees are yours"; or engineering appearing in the H5 exclusion
list - saying a thing is excluded IS saying who pays for it.
NEVER report this as missing where they have already excluded it. And never treat a business as
wrong for saying a wall MAY be structural or a permit MAY be needed - that is the correct thing to
say, and a business claiming a wall is definitely non-structural without looking is the one worth
flagging.

H7 - MINIMUM ATTENDANCE, INSPECTION AND CONSULTATION FEES, AND TRAVEL.
The smallest job they will come out for, what they charge to inspect or consult, and any travel
charge outside their area. A site inspection fee and a design consultation fee are both normal in
this trade and neither is a red flag.

H8 - SERVICE AREA AND GST.
A suburb or postcode they work out from AND how far they travel - "south-east Melbourne" is not a
service area. And whether their prices include GST, stated once, clearly.

H9 - PER-SQUARE-METRE, PER-ITEM AND HOURLY RATES - NOT BLOCKING.
Plastering at $65/m2, a door at $280 each, carpentry at $95 an hour. Real, useful, captured when
present, and NEVER a reason to reject on their own. A renovator who prices rooms and nothing else
has given us everything we need.

WARRANTY. Ask for how long their workmanship is warranted. Accept a plain answer in their own words;
never press for a number of years.

HOW TO APPLY THESE: work through H1 to H8 one at a time against what is actually written. Do not
form a general impression. A submission can read as thorough and confident while missing two of
these - a long catalogue of priced lines with no supply model attached to any of them, and nothing
said about permits, is exactly that shape. Judge only what is written; never fill a gap with what a
reasonable renovator probably does.

WHAT COUNTS AS QUOTABLE WORK HERE: a room, at a flat price, under a supply model.
Everything else - work by the square metre, work priced each, work by the hour or the day, delivery,
procurement fees, return attendance, variation administration, project management, site inspection
and consultation fees - is an optional add-on and NEVER blocking on its own. "From $850" on any of
those is perfectly acceptable and must not be reported as a problem.

WHAT IS WORTH ADDING (for alsoWorthAdding, never for fixes): the rooms they left out; the second
supply column if they only published one; their per-square-metre rates, which let a customer wanting
only new flooring be quoted at all; per-item door and cabinet prices; a project management fee for
larger jobs; their position on hidden damage found mid-job. A renovator with three rooms in one
supply model quotes almost nobody, and every customer who wanted the materials included passes them
by without either of them ever knowing.

GROUPING, WORKED FOR THIS TRADE. Same action, different items, ONE line:
  Wrong - three separate fixes:
    "Ensuite has no price."   "Laundry has no price."   "Hallway says POA."
  Right - one fix:
    "Add your price for renovating an ensuite, a laundry and a hallway - these are currently missing
     or POA, which we can't quote from."

--- background knowledge from here down: context, never a checklist ---

THE TWO MODELS: A renovator either supplies the materials and installs them, or installs what the
customer has bought. Both are normal and most businesses do both. Under installation-only the
customer is responsible for the materials being correct, complete, undamaged and the right size, and
a return visit because they were not is usually charged.

STRUCTURAL WORK: A wall is either load-bearing or it is not, and the difference is not visible from
a photograph. Removing a structural wall needs engineering, temporary support and usually a beam.
Nobody should tell a customer a wall can come out without assessing it, and open-plan conversions
are the commonest place this goes wrong.

HIDDEN CONDITIONS: Existing homes conceal water damage, rot, mould, termite damage, poor previous
workmanship, and plumbing and wiring where nobody expects it. These are found once demolition
starts, are not in the original quotation, and should be re-quoted rather than absorbed.

LICENSED TRADES: Electrical, plumbing and gas work must be done by somebody licensed for it.
A renovator can coordinate them; that is not the same as including them.

WET AREAS: Bathrooms, ensuites and laundries need waterproofing under the tiles, and it is the
single most expensive thing to get wrong because the failure shows up years later in another room.

PROCESS: Consult, inspect, scope, measure, quote, demolish, prepare, build, plaster, floor, fit
cabinetry, tile, paint, hang doors and trims, finish, inspect, hand over.

WHAT'S USUALLY INCLUDED: protection of the rest of the house, agreed demolition, surface
preparation, installation labour, finishing and clean-up. WHAT'S USUALLY NOT: permits, engineering,
council and architectural fees, electrical, plumbing, gas, asbestos removal, appliance supply,
specialist stone fabrication, major landscaping and anything hidden.

When answering, be upfront that exact figures always need a site-specific check - the room, its
condition, what is behind the walls, access and approvals all change the final price.
